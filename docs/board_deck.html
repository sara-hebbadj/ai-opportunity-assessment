Process diagnostic

Most waiting happens after approval, in the payment stage

  • The payment stage holds 56% of all waiting time; from payment request to payment takes 3.2 days at the median. Payments run on fixed days: a daily payment run is the fastest win, and needs no AI.
  • Supervisors are on 97% of claims and hold 17% of waiting; their p90 wait is 5.9 days.
  • After a rejection, employees take 1.0 days (median), 5.7 (p90) to respond.

Voice of staff

Voice of staff: an LLM codes interviews as consistently as the answer key

  • 12 interviews and 40 survey answers (synthetic), split into 120 snippets and coded into 8 themes by an LLM, one snippet per call.
  • Agreement with the theme each snippet was written for: gpt-6-luna κ 0.93, gemini-3.8-flash κ 0.96, claude-sonnet-5.5 κ 0.97 (n = 120). This is an upper bound: the same agent wrote the snippets and the labels. Sara's blind coding is pending.
  • Survey (n = 40, 1–5, synthetic): "I can see where my claim is" 2.0; "I would use a pre-check" 3.9.
  • What staff asked for: "warn, never block" and "flag the claim, not the person".
  • The synthetic snippets were written to cover the 8 themes about evenly, so the shares above do not rank pain points; with real interviews they would.

Pilot plan

Pilot: 6 weeks, two departments, with kill criteria agreed up front

WeekWhat happens
0Baseline rejection rate, data terms, UAE hosting choice, briefing for clerks
1Shadow mode: the pre-check runs, staff do not see it; clerks label every flag
2–5Live for the pilot group (warnings only); comparison group as today
3Checkpoint against the kill criteria
6Read-out and go / no-go

Stop the pilot if (any one):

  • flag precision on clerk-reviewed real claims is below 80% (week 1 or 3);
  • it misses more of the clerks' problems than the rules alone;
  • receipts leave the approved hosting, or any personal-data incident;
  • staff spend more than 2 extra minutes per claim on warnings;
  • by week 6 rejections are not trending towards the break-even reduction (42%).
